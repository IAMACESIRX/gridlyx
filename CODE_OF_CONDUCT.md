# Code of Conduct

## Standard

Participants are expected to keep technical discussion constructive, specific and evidence-oriented. Critique ideas, code, tests and architecture without attacking the person presenting them.

Acceptable participation includes:

- asking basic or advanced questions without ridicule;
- disagreeing with technical decisions using reproducible evidence or clearly stated reasoning;
- reporting bugs, limitations and security concerns in good faith;
- acknowledging uncertainty and correcting mistakes;
- respecting contributor privacy and boundaries;
- giving credit for code, research, assets and prior work.

Unacceptable participation includes harassment, threats, targeted abuse, discriminatory conduct, deliberate disruption, doxxing, credential exposure, malicious code submission, knowingly false security reports, or pressure to bypass project safety/provenance controls.

## Technical disputes

When a dispute concerns compatibility or implementation truth, prefer reproducible tests, authoritative upstream references and the repository's readiness/evidence model. A maintainer may request that speculative claims be labelled as assumptions until validated.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers may edit, hide, reject or remove contributions and participation that violate these expectations. Serious security, privacy or safety concerns should be routed privately where possible rather than litigated in public issue threads.

This conduct document governs participation in this project; it does not replace GitHub's own terms, policies or community guidelines.
